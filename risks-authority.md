# EmbArch: fleet risks — unattended authority

**Status:** active, 2026-09-09. **Split out of [risks.md](risks.md) on 2026-09-09** (`tasks/fleet/001`), which had reached 12,285 of its 12,288 B cap — **3 bytes** — with no `##` section to cut at. The entries below moved **verbatim**: a split restates nothing, so no argument was shortened to pay for room, which is [DOC-COMPACTION.md](../embarch-doc/DOC-COMPACTION.md) §2's default remedy. Read [risks.md](risks.md) first; it carries the register's own rule about when an entry may be removed.

Bare `§N` references below are sections of [protocol.md](protocol.md); anything else names its doc.

**The mission is one question: what may an agent do with nobody watching, and what did each grant cost?** It is the group most likely to grow — every future capability the owner grants adds an entry — and the entries share a shape the rest of the register does not: *granted on a date, by the owner, bounded by these things, and its characteristic failure looks like this.* That is why this is the seam rather than one of the two `##` sections left behind in `risks.md`.

- **An unattended agent now touches hardware, and flashes a client's board.** Granted by the owner 2026-09-06 (§7, §2). A `bench` task is run by the supervisor itself, serialized, after validating the roles it names — but it is still an agent flashing a DUT and running studies at 3am with nobody watching, on a board belonging to a client, and the suite has exactly one `hw_lock` and no queue behind it. What bounds it is narrow and worth stating plainly: **workers are still barred entirely**, one bench unit runs at a time, an unattached board leaves the task `open` rather than blocked, a topology mismatch stops the unit instead of being re-enrolled around, and **writing to a client repo is still reserved** — the grant is flash-and-study, not development. The characteristic failure is not a crash: it is a bench unit that quietly re-enrols past a mismatch, or asserts an inferred DUT fact as measured, and leaves a wrong hardware fact in a doc that later work trusts. Watch for a `**Decided:**` line that names a board fact no task supplied.
- **An agent now writes the instance's generated files, at a leg boundary.** `deploy.py --queue` pins a framework SHA and the listener spawns an `embarch-deployer` that runs `--from-latch`; that agent commits `.claude/`, the protocol READMEs and the shims into `embarch-doc` and pushes both repos. Until 2026-09-06 no agent could write those paths at all, and [ops](ops.md) §5.1's "there is no route from Slack to a standing rule" was true without qualification. **It is still true of authorship and that is the whole defence**: the deployer renders a commit the owner already made in a repo no leg ever checks out, any render input that moved past the pin is a refusal not a fresher deploy, and every liveness check except the pump latch applies. What was traded is that the *route* now exists where it did not — a rogue commit in `embarch-fleet` plus a rewritten latch would reach the instance unattended, where before it needed the owner's own hands. Bought for **~3.7 h of the ~6.6 h dead time** in the 14 h window ending 2026-09-06: the fleet stopped so the owner could hold the pen. Watch for a deploy landing the owner does not recognise from his own `--queue`, and for a red gate leaving the instance rendered-and-uncommitted longer than one boundary.
- **The supervisor executes cross-repo passes unattended.** §8's reasoning is sound and its blast radius is still the largest in the suite. Announce-and-park narrows the window rather than closing it: an owner who does not read it inside 30 minutes gets the change anyway, which is what full delegation means.
- **A worker's design authority is scoped by repo, not by consequence.** A one-repo change can be less reversible than a two-repo rename — a wire encoding, a retired decision, a deleted doc. Scope was chosen because it is mechanically checkable; where it is the wrong boundary will show up in [embarch-decision-reversals.md](../embarch-doc/embarch-decision-reversals.md).
- **The owner's session, the listener and the supervisor were one context until 2026-09-03.** It ran batches, listened to Slack, and wrote the rules the supervisor obeys — amending standing rules and `scripts/` repeatedly, legitimately, with nothing structural marking the boundary, which is how three "documentation shaped exactly like the data it documents" defects shipped and were then caught only by the machinery they broke. Split into three ([ops](ops.md) §8.1). **The residue is that a leg run inline, which the owner may still ask for, puts them back in one context.**
- **Work can start from Slack outside the protocol.** A message that is not a `fleet` command is acted on by a spawned agent with the owner's authority ([ops](ops.md) §5.3) — no task file, no ownership map, no gate. Chosen deliberately for the reach it buys; it means the three mechanisms §3 and §10 exist to enforce have a documented way around them, bounded only by who sent the message.
